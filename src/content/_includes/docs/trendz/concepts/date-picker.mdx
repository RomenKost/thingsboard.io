The **date picker** sets the time range a view works with: Trendz only loads and analyzes data that falls inside this range. You find it in the toolbar above every view, and the same picker is used wherever Trendz asks for a period — in Anomaly Detection, Prediction, Vibration Analysis, and the Metric Explorer.

A new view starts with **This Week**, in your browser's time zone.

## Absolute and relative ranges

A time range is either absolute or relative:

- An **absolute** range has fixed start and end dates, like *August 1 – August 10, 2026*. It always shows the same period, no matter when you open the view.
- A **relative** range is described in relation to the current moment, like *last 7 days* or *this month*. It moves forward with time: a view set to **Last 7 days** shows a different week tomorrow than today.

Use a relative range for views you open regularly and expect to show recent data, like monitoring dashboards. Use an absolute range to analyze a specific period, like an incident or a billing month.

A relative range can also point to the **future** — **Tomorrow**, **Next 7 days**, **Next Month**. This is useful for views that show forecasts or planned values.

## Open the date picker

Click the date picker in the view toolbar. The picker has three parts:

- **Presets** on the left — ready-made ranges like **Today** or **Last 30 days**.
- **Use relative** / **Use absolute** under the presets — switches the right-hand side between the calendars and the relative range builder.
- **Timezone** under the presets — opens the time zone settings.

Every change applies immediately — there is no **Apply** button. The picker closes when you pick a preset, press **Esc**, or click outside it.

## Presets

Presets are the fastest way to pick a common range. They are grouped by unit; hover over or click a group to open it. The preset that matches the current range is highlighted.

| Group | Presets |
|-------|---------|
| — | Today, Yesterday, Tomorrow |
| **Days** | Last 7 days, Last 14 days, Last 30 days, Last 90 days, Next 7 days, Next 14 days, Next 30 days |
| **Week** | This Week, Prev Week, Next Week, Last 2 weeks, Last 3 weeks, Last 4 weeks |
| **Month** | This Month, Prev Month, Next Month, Last 3 months, Last 6 months, Last 12 months |
| **Year** | This Year, Last year, Prev Year, Next Year, Last 3 years |

How the presets count time:

- **Last N** ranges include the current period. **Last 7 days** is the 6 previous days plus today.
- **Next N** ranges also start with the current period. **Next 7 days** is today plus the 6 following days.
- **This**, **Prev**, and **Next** cover exactly one whole calendar unit — the current, the previous, or the following one.
- **Last year** is the last 365 days up to today, while **Prev Year** is the previous calendar year.
- Weeks start on Monday.

All presets are relative ranges.

## Set an absolute range

The picker shows two calendars side by side. Use the arrows or the month and year dropdowns to move between months.

<Steps>
1. Click the first day of the range.

2. Click the last day of the range. Both days are included.

3. Optionally, set the exact time in the **From** and **To** fields under the calendars — hours, minutes, and seconds.
</Steps>

## Set a custom relative range

When no preset fits, build your own relative range. Click **Use relative** to open the **Custom relative range** builder. It has two rows — **From** for the start of the range and **To** for its end. In each row, choose:

- **Direction** — **last** (in the past), **this** (the current period), or **next** (in the future).
- **Amount** — how many units. Not used with **this**.
- **Unit** — minute, hour, day, week, month, quarter, or year.
- **Alignment** — **from its start** rounds to the beginning of the unit, like midnight for a day; **at this exact time** keeps the current time of day.

Examples:

| From | To | Result |
|------|----|--------|
| last 3 days, from its start | this day, from its start | The 3 days before today, without today |
| this day, from its start | next 3 days, from its start | Today and the next 2 days |
| last 6 hours, at this exact time | this hour, at this exact time | The 6 hours up to the current moment |

## See the exact time range

For a relative range, the date picker in the toolbar shows its name — like **Last 7 days** — rather than dates. To see the exact dates and times it covers right now:

- Open the picker: the calendars highlight the days the range currently covers.
- Click **Use relative**: the **Resolves to** line under the builder shows the exact start and end, down to the second, in the selected time zone.

For an absolute range, the toolbar shows the dates directly, like `Aug.01.2026 - Aug.10.2026`, and adds times when they are set.

## Change the time zone

By default, the date picker uses your browser's time zone. Change it when the data belongs to a site in another time zone — for example, to see "today" as it is for a plant in another country.

<Steps>
1. Open the date picker and click **Timezone**.

2. Open the time zone dropdown and start typing a city or region name, like `Kyiv` or `America`, to filter the list.

3. Select the time zone. The view reloads with the new time zone.

4. Click **Timezone** again to return to the range settings.
</Steps>

Under the dropdown, the picker shows the UTC offset of the selected time zone, whether it observes daylight saving time, and your browser's own time zone for comparison.

When you change the time zone of an absolute range, the selected calendar days stay the same — the range now means those days in the new time zone.

## Related settings

- **group by** — next to the date picker, sets how the time range is split into intervals on charts. See <DocLink product={props.product} path="group-by-time">Grouping by time</DocLink>.
- **Hide date picker** — hides the date picker and the **group by** control when the view is shown on a dashboard or in full-screen mode. See <DocLink product={props.product} path="view-settings">View settings</DocLink>.
- **Filter by date** — narrows the data inside the time range to certain hours, weekdays, or months. See <DocLink product={props.product} path="data-filtering">Data filtering</DocLink>.
